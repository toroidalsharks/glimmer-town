# History

Glimmer Town grew one version at a time as a single HTML file, each version patched on top of the last. In v26 the file was split into `src/` so it can be edited like a normal project. The split itself lost nothing: before any v26 fix went in, building `src/` gave back the v25 file byte for byte.

The files in `src/js` are roughly in the order things were added.

| Version | What came in | Where it lives now |
|---|---|---|

| early | The 3D town, resident models, interiors, happiness and presents, dreams, mail, the ferry and the second island, festivals, the morning paper, building, visitors, synthesized sound | `200` to `310` |
| middle | Red and invited residents, downtown, love and weddings, fishing, seasons, birthdays, your garden plot, the camera, Glimmer Hall court, books, jobs and strikes, GlimmerNet, Claude's workshop | `320` to `440` |

## Nightly changes

- 2026-09-26: Put the project folders back after a phone upload flattened them. At Milo's request, Mili now wears a wispy, medium-length layered pixie cut (a new "Pixie" style anyone can pick), a one-time fix restores it on existing saves, and random morning haircuts no longer touch real people (Mili, Red, Tim, invited residents). Two new smoke checks cover both.
- 2026-09-26: Styles, faces and uniforms. Every resident has a personality (energy, warmth, edge, tidiness, love of cute things) and a clothing style that grows out of it: casual, preppy, sporty, streetwear, emo, goth, alt, jirai kei, gyaru, lolita, cottagecore, Y2K, classic, mom or dad. Faces match (sleepy, sharp, sparkly or dot eyes, lashes, eyeliner, under-eye blush, freckles, cat mouths, resting frowns). Everyone changes into a uniform for their job. Residents comment on each other's outfits, bond over shared styles, and sometimes start dressing like their best friend; the AI minds now know each resident's vibe and what everyone is wearing. Real people start in a plain style and only change when Milo picks one.
- 2026-09-26: Murders now happen about once a real year instead of once a game year (every couple of hours); the Court tab has a picker for it. A grieving detective hands the case to an acting detective. Misleading clues get a follow-up that crosses them out, every clue is labeled (evidence, witness, rumor, ruled out), suspects show how much evidence points at them, and a Detective's briefing scene walks through the case (it also plays by itself halfway through serious cases). Text threads have an exit button at the top and bottom. Auto-update now checks the published page itself before reloading, so an out-of-step version.json can't cause reload loops.
- 2026-09-26: Sentencing. After a guilty verdict the Creator picks a sentence that fits the crime (community service in an orange vest, a public apology, a warning, the cell, probation with an ankle monitor, anger management, stay-away orders, losing a job for fraud; for murder a real year with monthly parole hearings, life, or exile). Parole hearings play as a court scene with the victim's loved one. Wrongly convicted people are paid back. AI-written trial and questioning lines can no longer state facts: they only carry feelings and arguments, and a filter drops any line that mentions times, places, colors, objects or people outside the case.
- 2026-09-26: Look studio. Every resident card has a Change look button near the top (the old style picker was a collapsed row halfway down the card, and it vanished when the cute look was off). It has four tabs: Style, Clothes, Hair and Face. There are 12 new styles (comfy, business, dark academia, coquette, boho, fairy kei, kidcore, neko with cat ears and a tail, e-girl/e-boy, grunge, witchy, techwear), for 27 in all, and every piece can be changed on its own: layers (flannel, puffer, tech jacket, corset, cape, overalls and the old ones), bottoms (cargo, ripped jeans, plaid skirt, tutu), tights, shoes, platforms, hats and hair pieces (beanie, witch hat, cat ears, flower crown, ribbon, star clips, headphones), glasses or sunglasses, neckwear, bags, bangles, a tail, leg warmers and shirt prints. Faces gained eye shapes (starry, cat, droopy, wide, tiny), eye colors, fang and tongue-out mouths, grin and small mouths, brow and blush controls, lipstick, stickers, a bandage, a beauty mark and piercings, plus skin tone and ears. Face changes stay put across style changes, and kids only get kid styles and no makeup. A one-time upgrade moved a few residents into the new styles, and now and then a resident swaps a piece of their outfit on their own. Six new smoke checks.
- 2026-09-26: The workshop's color picker kept jumping back to mint, because the panel redraws itself every few seconds and the dropdown forgot what you chose. It's now a row of color dots that remembers your pick, and every "Make anything" card shows that color. One new smoke check.
- 2026-09-27: Glimmer 2, a low-poly diorama look for the town and a redesign of every menu (the layout stays). A first version with cel bands, ink lines and one pastel palette was rejected: the shading looked strange and the residents lost their own colors and detail. So the style now lives in the town's shapes and leaves the residents alone. With `FACET.on` (`545-art-style.js`, decided at startup), every town material is flat-shaded, `sph()` and `cyl()` use fewer segments, the island and beach rings have fewer sides, `clumpCanopy()` rebuilds broadleaf crowns as two or three stacked rounded clumps with scalloped edges and warm dark undersides (like the olive trees in a paper diorama), `ballCanopy()` makes blossom trees (the `treeC` stand-in, picked by `treeParts()` for pink crowns) and bushes into clusters of faceted balls, pines keep their cones and stay darker because `moodLeaf()` keeps each leaf color's lightness, and `facetGeo()` snaps the rocks and clouds to a coarse grid, so every facet has one color. Roofs, walls, paths and sand keep a softened version of their shingles, planks and cobbles, glass and glowing things (lamp globes, berries, the fountain) stay smooth, garden crops are leafy sprouts, the fountain spray is small white drops, palm fronds take the season's leaf color, and the lawn is calmer, with no blade strokes and 28% of the tufts, shorter and yellow-green. A second version with pointy stacked tiers on every tree and plain roofs was judged too samey and bare, which is why the trees have three shapes again and the textures came back. Residents, Mochi, Judge Hoot and the ghosts are built inside `smoothly()`, so they keep their smooth shapes, and `toon()` gives them their own `'res|'` materials. Settings has a Town look picker: Meadow (the new default: pale yellow-green grass, chartreuse trees, tan rocks, a bright aqua sea, a lower sun for longer shadows, a stronger sky light for soft ones, and v24's color grade so residents stay true), Golden hour (peach haze, lavender water, yellow-ochre trees, calmer sand and a low sun), Candy, Hologram, and Storybook (the old smooth look; switching to or from it reloads the box). Each look also recolors the menus. The menus were redesigned: tabs with icons, raised pill buttons, softer cards, a frosted HUD, a new dialog frame. The Look studio gained 10 styles (Decora, Balletcore, Angelcore, Mermaidcore, Skater, Beach day, Western, Tennis club, Idol, Cyber Y2K) for 37 in all. It also gained 8 haircuts (hime cut, wavy, ponytail, wolf cut, mohawk, space buns, braids, afro) and 5 hair colors. New pieces: layers (bomber, metallic jacket, sailor collar, ballet wrap), flares and a ruffle skirt, hats and hair pieces (sun hat, cowboy hat, visor, bandana, bunny ears, shell clips, tiara, glowing halo, stage headset), pearls, scarves and kerchiefs, wings, a skateboard, a plush bear, a light stick, and heart shades or a shiny visor. No one's outfit changes on its own: the new styles show up in the Look studio and on newcomers. Also fixed a sign bug that left some residents' hair color undefined (it showed as "undefined hair" in the Look studio). Three new smoke checks.
- 2026-09-26: Custody and smoother menus. Anyone charged with a crime now waits for trial in the cell under Glimmer Hall instead of walking around town, and a trial that got lost because the box reloaded mid-queue is held again the next morning (or at boot). A verdict whose sentence never got picked uses the default sentence. Mail: going back from a letter returns you to the same spot in the list, open letters have newer/older arrows, swiping, arrow keys and a "Next unread" button. Letters come at most two a night, small ones no more than one per resident every few days and never the same kind twice in a row, each kind has several wordings, and most letters end with a P.S. from the resident's own day (their job, a friend, their outfit, a question for the Creator). The AI letter writer sees their recent days and their last letters so it doesn't repeat them. Scenes no longer close the panel while you're dressing someone or reading mail (the panel counts as busy for 4 minutes in those, 90 seconds otherwise, up from 25), and a panel a scene did close comes back afterwards. The tab row is one scrolling line instead of four wrapped rows, and a ⤢ button makes the panel taller. Five new smoke checks.
- 2026-09-27: Starline, a busy city quarter across a bridge east of downtown (`335-starline.js`). It opens when 15 people live in town or on day 40, whichever comes first. It has Starline Tower (rooms 22 to 25), Starlight Cinema, the Glimmer FM radio station, three food trucks with a night market every third evening, Beat Box dance studio, a monorail over the avenue that runs while its conductor is on shift, and cars circling the plaza. Five new jobs come with it (conductor, food truck cook, projectionist, radio host, dance teacher), and the first few are filled by generated residents; real people keep their jobs. The town now holds 25 residents at most: births, move-ins, invites and ferry visitors all stop at the cap. A new test, `tests/city.mjs`, covers it.
- 2026-09-27: Starline has to be earned now (`337-starline-build.js`). At 15 residents or day 40 the plans go up at the fountain, with a notice board on downtown's east lawn and buoys out at sea marking the plot. The Creator pays 3000 coins, 40 wood, 30 stone, 15 metal parts and 12 flowers from the Build tab, a bit at a time, and residents who like the idea drop coins in the jar. Everyone has an opinion and a reason (a cinema, jobs, rooms and bustle on one side; the shops, the shore, the sky, noise, traffic, prices and change on the other), and the diary follows the arguments, a petition, rumors that get checked and ruled out, and mixed feelings. Once it's paid for, a hearing at the fountain ends with the Creator making one promise (green space, local jobs first, or quiet nights), the first shovel goes in and the land rises out of the sea. A crew of up to four generated residents, builders first, builds it shift by shift over about five days: foundations, then each building rising inside its scaffolding under two turning cranes, dust, a topping-out tree on the tower, a monorail track, then signs, lights and trees. Petition signers rally at the bridge, the crew digs up a find that stops work for a day, storms shut the site, and the Creator can lend a hand once a day for 3 wood and 3 stone. A ribbon cutting with fireworks opens it, and the promise changes things (more trees, the crew hired first, or shifts that end at sunset). Opinions follow the U-shaped curve seen around real projects: most people who were against it come round as it goes up and after it opens. Real people never lead or sign petitions, argue on the record, rally, or join the crew. Old saves where Starline opened but nobody lived or worked there go back to plans; the others stay open.
- 2026-09-27: Laptops stay. A resident's room holds 12 things, and a new one used to push out the oldest, which was usually the laptop, so laptops vanished a few days after they were bought or given. Now a laptop never gets pushed out, the AI minds are told when a resident owns one, and a one-time fix gives every grown-up in an existing town a laptop. Love keeps going while the box is off: the away catch-up now has confessions, dates starting, proposals, breakups and divorce requests (same rules as live, real people included only where they already are), and a wedding due that day actually happens. Two new smoke checks.
- 2026-09-27: Cutscene lines no longer get cut off. Tapping while a line was typing out stopped the typing but never showed the rest, so the line stayed cut wherever the tap landed. Scenes also repeat themselves less: every scene line now comes from a picker that remembers the last 400 lines said and chooses one nobody has heard lately, and trials got many more lines (six or so for each part, and several objections and rulings instead of one). Two new smoke checks.
- 2026-10-05: No cutscene line is ever shown twice. The town remembers the last 6000 lines it heard in scenes, and a line that was said before comes out reworded for whoever says it (a resident adds an opener or an ending, Judge Hoot hoots or adjusts his spectacles, the narrator adds a quiet beat, the crowd murmurs). The added words are feelings and filler only, never facts, so cases stay solvable. One new smoke check.
- 2026-10-06: Murders come from the town's history. Nothing schedules a murder or picks a killer at random anymore. A murder can only happen after a feud has run for 21 or more game days, kept raw by real fights and slights, and the one holding the grudge gets a night where they can turn back (most do; friends, a partner, therapy and a good bond with the Creator make it likelier). The Court tab setting is now a cap. The dead and the banished are kept in the Court tab with their own words about themselves, who they were closest to and what they remembered most. Residents can step away from a talk with the Creator, and that holds for about half an hour. Creator talks also ask for a private thought, which the Creator glimpses about one time in four. The talk prompt no longer lists feelings residents should have about the Creator. Three new smoke checks.
